SUMMARY

This publication consists of a soil map (2 sheets) at a scale of 1:20 000 covering the county of Chambly and a report describing the soils, their genesis and suitability for agriculture. The present study represents a modern survey to replace the original work by Baril and Mailloux in 1942.

The study area covers 35 418 hectares in the St. Lawrence Lowlands, southeast of Montreal. It is located in the St. Lawrence and Richelieu drainage basins between 45° 22′ and 45° 39′ N Lat and between 73° 14′ and 73° 31′ W Long.

The level plain is composed of marine, fluvial and estuarine materials over morainal deposits of variable thickness. These materials were deposited on sedimentary rock before, during and after the Champlain sea epoch (about 12 000 BP). Clay soils cover 50.7 % of the area, loamy soils cover 8.4 %, sandy and skeletal (stony) soils cover 1 % and 2 % respectively. Urban and industrial uses cover 30.5 % of the area.

The climate is temperate continental with a growing season of about 208 days and a frost free period of 169 days. Over 50 % of the county is farmed, mainly on the clay soils. Forest consisting mainly of birch, aspen and red maple cover less than 15 % of the county, mainly on the poorer sandy and shallow to bedrock soils.

In all, 59 soil series and variants were defined and described according to the standards of the Canadian System of Soil Classification. The soil map contains 1 303 polygons grouped into 176 map units, of which 16 are non-soils covering 40 % of the land area. Wetness is the main limitation to agricultural capability but with drainage many of the clays are class 2 with the loams at class 3.
