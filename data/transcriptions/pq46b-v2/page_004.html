SUMMARY

The soils of Richelieu county have been differentiated according to their landscape position, their particle size, the thickness of their parent materials, their drainage, and their genetic development. Variants are based on differences in carbonate content and texture of the substratum. In all, 49 soil series and variants were defined and described according to the standards of the Canadian System of Soil Classification.

The parent materials originate mainly from marine sediments related to the Champlain Sea and pre-Saint-Lawrence epochs. They are generally well sorted, are occasionally stratified, and occur dominantly on flat or gently sloping land. Poorly drained conditions prevail.

Sandy soils occupy the largest area in the county. They are subdivided into two groups, either shallow (<75 cm) or deep over a contrasting substratum. The soils of Joseph, Prairie, Massueville, Achigan, and Saint-Thomas series are mainly fine sand. Medium sand is dominant in the Saint-Jude and Sainte-Sophie series. The Fleury and Michaudville series have a loamy substratum, whereas those of the Aston and Saint-Damase series have a clayey substratum. Moreover, they are differentiated on the basis of their genetic development and drainage.

Soils developed in loamy materials are represented by the Saint-Aimé, Saint-Ours, Bellevue, Sainte-Rose, and Pierreville series. All these soils are classified as Gleysols, whereas those of the Yamaska and Du Bourrelet series are Podzols. Most soils of the Lac Saint-Pierre lowlands are loamy.

Clayey, deep and poorly drained Orthic Humic Gleysols occupy 17 % of the county. The important series representing these soils are Saint-Urbain, Sainte-Rosalie, and Providence (very fine-clayey) as well as Kierkoski and Des Chenaux (fine-clayey). They are differentiated on the basis of the permeability, consistency, reduction, and mottling of the profile.

Organic soils occupy less than 2 % of Richelieu county. The Sainte-Victoire and Tracy series are the most important ones of this group. Organic soils are separated according to the degree of decomposition and the thickness of the organic layers over the mineral substratum.
