SUMMARY

The soil survey of Saint-Jean county is the second soil inventory. A semi-detailed survey (1 : 63 360) was first completed in 1942 by Theriault, Pageau and Mailloux. The decision to survey these soils of the Montreal lowlands, at a scale 1 : 40 000, was taken in response to a strong regional demand for more precise soil information. This document is aimed at users of these resource who want to better understand the nature, variability, potential and limitations of the soils. This technical bulletin comprises a report and a soil map printed at the scale 1 : 40 000.

The Saint-Jean county is located to the south-east of the Montreal plain in the St. Lawrence lowlands physiographic region. It covers an area of 467.15 km² and most of the land is used for agriculture (67%), urban and industrial development (17%) while a small proportion is under forest (< 25%). Regional climatic conditions are amongst the most favourable in the province of Quebec. They are characterized by moderately warm summers and moderately cold winters, mean annual precipitation between 900 to 1 000 mm, growing degree-days ranging between 1 939 and 2 311 and a growing season over 200 days which allows growing a wide range of crops. The main crops are: corn for grain, soybean, alfalfa, and forage crops. About 1% of the agricultural area is used for horticultural crops (sweet-corn, tomato, cucumber, etc.).

The soils of Saint-Jean county lie on a geologic base made of sedimentary rocks from the Ordovician epoch. After the last glaciation, more than 13 000 years B.P., a mantle of glacial deposits of varying thickness (0.5 to 6 m) covered the regional bedrock. The northward retreat of the glacier, the incursion of the St. Lawrence lowlands by the sea followed by isostatic uplift created different stages of water regression (Chambly pro-glacial lake, Champlain sea, Lampsilis lake, Montréal lake, pre-St. Lawrence, etc.) which influenced the erosion, transport and deposition modes of the sediments in Saint-Jean county. Thus we find mainly more or less reworked tills (24.1%), glacial-lacustrine (2.8%), marine (26.9%), estuarine or lacustrine (10.1%), fluvial (19.2%), fluvio-lacustrine (4.9%) and alluvial soils (4.2%). Wetlands make up 6.4% of the classified soils. These organic materials are located in channels or in depressions, the latter forming basin bogs or shore swamps.

Survey, mapping and soil classification activities were carried out on all the land except for urban or industrial areas (5 659 ha). A stratified random sampling strategy was used where close to 2 100 sites were described at intervals varying from 100 to 400 m and recorded within 232 transects. The average inspection density is 1 observation per 22.3 ha. The final soil legend includes 51 taxonomic units (soil series). The map contains 498 polygons grouped into 81 map units. Typically, the soils of Saint-Jean county are gleysolic (74.5%), loamy (39.2%) or clayey (35.1%), alkaline (56.4%) or neutral (31.8%) and strongly calcareous (52.4%).

Most of the soils of Saint-Jean county fall into agricultural capability class two (19.5%) or three (56.7%) of the Canada Land Inventory (CLI). The main limitations are: excess water as a result of poor drainage (75.7%), undesirable structure and/or slow permeability of the surface layer and/or sub-soil (40.8%), low natural fertility (15.4%), a relief susceptible to water erosion or limiting the use of farm machinery (14.7%), areas occasionnaly floodded (5.7%) and slight to moderate surface stoniness (5.4%).

Under intensive agriculture, the soils of Saint-Jean county could be slightly to moderately affected by acidification (58.2%), compaction (77.1%), loss of organic matter (88.0%), erodibility (87.6%), water erosion (81.3%) or wind erosion (67.9%). A high percentage of the soils is moderately to highly susceptible to surface crusting (95.0%).

Better knowledge of soils should allow the users and the managers of this non renewable resource to insure their maintenance and sustainability for future generations.
