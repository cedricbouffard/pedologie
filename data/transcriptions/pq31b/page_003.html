SUMMARY

The soil survey of Laprairie county is the second soil inventory. A semi-detailed survey (1 : 63 360) was first completed in 1943 by Theriault and Godbout. The decision to re-survey the soils of the Montreal lowlands at a more detailed scale (1 : 20 000) was taken in response to a strong regional demand for more precise soil information. This document is aimed at users of the soil resource who want to better understand the nature, variability, potential and limitations of the soils. This technical bulletin comprises a memoir and a soil map printed at the scale of 1 : 20 000 on three map sheet.

The Laprairie county is located to the south-east of the Montreal plain in the St.Lawrence lowlands physiographic region. It covers an area of 438,84 km² and most of the land is used for agriculture (47%), urban and industrial development (18%) while a small proportion is under forest (< 25%). Regional climatic conditions are amongst the most favourable in the province of Quebec. They are characterized by moderately warm summers and moderately cold winters, mean annual precipitation between 900 to 1 000 mm, growing degree-days ranging between 1 939 and 2 125 and a growing season of over 200 days which allows growing a wide range of crops. The main crops are: corn for grain, soybean, alfalfa, and forage crops. About 3% of the agricultural area is used for horticultural crops (sweet-corn, tomato, cucumber, etc.).

The soils of Laprairie county lie on a geologic base made of sedimentary rocks from the Ordovician epoch. After the last glaciation, more than 13 000 years before present, a mantle of glacial deposits of varying thickness (0,5 - 6 m) covered the regional bedrock. The northward retreat of the glacier, the incursion of the St.Lawrence lowlands by the sea followed by isostatic uplift created different stages of water regression (Chambly pro-glacial lake, Champlain sea, Lampsilis lake, pre-St.Lawrence, etc.) which influenced the erosion, transport and deposition modes of the sediments in Laprairie county. Thus one finds mainly more or less reworked tills loamy or loamy-skeletal (14,7%), glacial-lacustrine clayey soils (4,1%), marine clayey (10,4%) or sandy soils (5,8%), estuarine or lacustrine clayey and/or stratified silty soils (1,7%), fluvial clayey soils (51,6%) and old or recent loamy alluvial soils (1,6%). Wetlands make up 3% of the classified soils. These organic materials are located in channels or in depressions, the latter forming basin bogs or shore swamps.

Survey, mapping and soil classification activities were carried out on all the land except for urban areas (7 765 ha) and Kahnawake (4 962 ha). A stratified random sampling strategy was used where close to 6 000 sites were described at intervals varying from 75 to 200 m and recorded within 421 transects. The average inspection density is 1 observation per 5,2 ha. The final soil legend includes 52 taxonomic units (soil series or variants). The map contains 1 399 polygons grouped into 151 map units. Typically, the soils of Laprairie county are gleysolic (86,2%), clayey (69,1%) or loamy (13,9%), alkaline (54,7%) or neutral (36,1%), highly calcareous (45,3%) or non calcareous (38,6%).

Most of the soils of Laprairie county fall into agricultural capability class two or three of the Canada Land Inventory system (CLI). The main limitations are: excess water as a result of poor drainage (92,7%), undesirable structure and/or slow permeability of the surface layer and/or sub-soil (78,7%), slight to moderate surface stoniness (24,1%), a relief susceptible to water erosion or limiting the use of farm machinery (11,6%), lack of moisture during dry spells (10,5%) and low natural fertility (9,9%).

Under intensive agriculture, the soils of Laprairie county could be slightly to moderately affected by acidification (25,7%), loss of organic matter (93,3%), erodibility (94,7%), water erosion (52,7%) or wind erosion (28,2%). A high percentage of the soils is moderately or moderately to highly susceptible to surface crusting (80,7%) and compaction (87,4%).

Better knowledge of soils should allow the users and the managers of this non renewable resource to insure their maintenance and sustainability for future generations.
