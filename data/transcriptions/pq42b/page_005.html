SUMMARY

This soil survey of Napierville county is the second soil inventory for this county. A semi-detailed survey (1: 63 360) was first completed in 1943 by Laplante and Alarie. The decision to re-survey the soils of the Montreal lowlands at a semi-detailed scale (1:40 000) was made in response to a strong regional demand for more precise soil information. This document is aimed at users of the soil resource who want to better understand the nature, variability, potential and limitations of the soils. This technical bulletin includes a report and a soil map printed at a scale of 1:40 000.

The Napierville county is located to the south-east of the Montreal plain in the St. Lawrence lowlands physiographic region. It covers an area of 387,7 km² and most of the land is used for agriculture (87,5 %), urban and industrial development (< 5 %) while a small proportion is under forest (< 10 %). Regional climatic conditions are amongst the most favourable for agriculture in the province of Quebec. They are characterized by moderately warm summers and moderately cold winters, mean annual precipitation close to 1 000 mm, growing degree-days ranging between 1 939 and 2 125 and a growing season of over 200 days which allows growing a wide range of crops. The main crops are: corn for grain, soybean, alfalfa, and forage crops. About 20 % of the agricultural area is used for horticultural crops (lettuce, carrot, onion, sweet-corn, tomato, potato, etc.).

The soils of Napierville county lie on a geologic base made of sedimentary rocks from the Ordovician epoch. After the last glaciation, more than 13 000 years B.P., a mantle of glacial deposits of varying thickness (0,5 - 6 m) covered the regional bedrock. The northward retreat of the glacier, the incursiom of the St. Lawrence lowlands by the sea, and subsequent isostatic uplift created different stages of water regression (Chambly pro-glacial lake, Champlain sea, Lampsilis lake, Montreal lake, pre-St. Lawrence, etc.) which influenced the erosion, transport and deposition modes of the sediments in Napierville county. Thus, we find mainly reworked tills (37,0 %), glacial-lacustrine (1,9 %), marine (18,2 %), estuarine or lacustrine (1,1 %), fluvial (16,8 %), fluvio-lacustrine (< 1 %) and alluvial soils (6,5 %). Wetlands make up 16,8 % of the classified soils. These organic materials are located in channels or in depressions, the latter forming basin bogs or shore swamps.

Survey, mapping and soil classification activities were carried out on all of the land except for urban and industrial areas (1 253,7 ha). A stratified random sampling strategy was used where close to 2 267 sites were described at intervals varying from 100 to 400 m and recorded within 205 transects. The average inspection density is 1 observation per 17,0 ha. The final soil legend includes 55 taxonomic units (soil series). The map contains 577 polygons grouped into 80 map units. Typically, the soils of Napierville county are gleysolic (58,6 %), loamy (32,7 %) or loamy-skeletal (25,0 %), alkaline (76,7 %) and strongly calcareous (83,0 %).

Most of the soils of Napierville county (37 203,0 ha) fall into agricultural capability class two (7,5 %) or three (67,3 %) of the Canada Land Inventory system (CLI). The main limitations are: excess water as a result of poor drainage (59,9 %), undesirable structure and/or slow permeability of the surface layer and/or sub-soil (45,0 %), low natural fertility (25,0 %), a relief susceptible to water erosion or limiting the use of farm machinery (21,4 %), areas occasionally flooded (0,4 %) and surface stoniness (25,0 %).

Under intensive agriculture, the soils of Napierville county could be slightly to moderately affected by acidification (54,0 %) and water erosion (71,4 %) or wind erosion (39,8 %). A significant percentage of the soils is moderately susceptible to surface crusting (49,5 %), compaction (47,8 %), loss of organic matter (53,3 %) and erodibility (48,6 %).

Better knowledge of soils should allow the users and the managers of this non-renewable resource to insure their maintenance and sustainability for future generations.
